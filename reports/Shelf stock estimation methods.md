# Anchoring stock predictions to calibrated per-area references

No published method grades fresh-produce fill as empty/half/full, so no off-the-shelf model will beat the current 78.8% (compliance 91%, stock 66%) by itself; the evidence supports a short list of cheap, decision-aligned changes instead. The costliest failure is predicting "full" over crate floor that is visible, which costs -3 each time, and the scoring arithmetic shows the fix is mostly calibration: full beats half only when p_full exceeds 4/7 of the half-plus-full probability mass, a threshold that can be tuned directly on out-of-fold (OOF) predictions with two parameters. The strongest adjacent evidence says the signal to extract is "fraction of the area that is empty" via comparison with a per-area reference, which fixed-camera work reached with a pixel-occupancy ratio and few-shot patch banks. For the Gemini Flash-Lite VLM, the best-supported moves are same-area labelled exemplars, evidence fields before the verdict, vote fractions instead of verbalised confidence, and packing 4-6 areas per request to fit 15 requests per minute. Label noise at the half/full boundary is best handled by re-inspecting model-flagged cases and soft targets, not by exotic losses. Temporal and transductive tricks are real but small with only 26 later-evening frames. The hidden set cannot resolve small gains: a 95% interval on a 90% accuracy over 289 areas is about plus or minus 3.5 points. One gap is stated plainly: frozen foundation embeddings (DINOv2, CLIP, SigLIP via ONNX or OpenCV dnn without torch) were not researched, so this report makes no claims about them beyond what the patch-bank literature implies.

## The literature grades empty space, not produce fill

Published out-of-stock work is almost entirely supervised detection of "empty space" boxes on close-up packaged-goods photos. Sikic et al. trained YOLOv5 on 511 images and 1,210 instances and reached **mAP 85.0%** (86.3 AP fully empty, 83.7 AP "front" empty with product left at the back), while product-detection baselines managed only 56.9-63.8% AP ([Sensors 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10819825)). That approach needs box labels, and our labels are area-level three-class, so it does not transfer directly. A late-2025 detector that adds a monocular depth branch (Depth Anything V2 pseudo-labels) reports +3.7% mAP from auxiliary learning and +4.2% from depth normalisation, but it is a trained network ([arXiv 2510.16508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.16508)). Only one method is closer to our hardware: NEC's ceiling-mounted fixed camera used background subtraction plus a learned change classifier, defined availability as the **ratio of product-occupied pixels to monitoring-area pixels**, and put 89.6% of samples within plus or minus 0.10, against 42.9% when every change was treated as a removal ([Sensors 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6631981/)). The gain came from classifying what changed, not from detecting that something changed, and it was for packaged beverages only. For produce, the one quantitative result is a depth-camera occupancy grid with about 5% maximum average discrepancy ([Milella et al., IEEE Access 2020](https://doaj.org/article/302301b555c1481b8a0ef957710e06f5)); we have no depth hardware. No source reports empty/half/full accuracy for produce from RGB, a half-versus-full confusion matrix, or lighting drift through a day. Our roughly 1,900 labels are therefore likely the best fill-level produce set available, and external data is useful at most for pretraining features.

The most transferable idea is a per-area reference bank. AnomalyDINO stores DINOv2 patch features of a few nominal images and scores a test patch by nearest-neighbour cosine distance, reaching **96.6 image AUROC at 1-shot on MVTec-AD versus 83.4 for PatchCore**, and 87.4 versus 79.9 on VisA ([arXiv 2405.14529](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14529v3)). Its authors state it targets low-level defects (a semantic "cable swap" scored near chance), the benchmarks are industrial, and no source applies it to shelves or compares it with histogram kNN. The reference-purity result matters for us: adding 0.5% anomalous patches to a clean bank cut AUPRC from 0.1030 to 0.0759, so a "full" bank should be built only from human-confirmed full labels ([arXiv 2608.22082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22082)). My adaptation, not a published result, is to score each crop by the **fraction of its patches that are far from the area's "full" bank**, which maps naturally onto the visible-crate-floor error. An OpenCV/numpy version of the same logic (Lab colour means, LBP or HOG per grid cell, kNN against the full bank and an empty-crate-floor bank) follows the identical mechanism with no torch, though no published figure backs its gain. The AnomalyDINO route itself needs a ViT-S/14 forward pass, roughly 21M parameters. Whether that runs acceptably through ONNX or OpenCV dnn on CPU is exactly the unresearched embeddings question, and the notes cannot answer it.

## The cost matrix makes half versus full a 4:3 ratio test

The scoring determines the decision geometry, and the arithmetic (derived from the project scoring, not cited literature) is sharper than it looks. With probabilities summing to one, EV(empty) = 3p_e - 2 and EV(full) = 4p_f - 3. Adjacent choices are ratio tests: half beats empty when 3p_h exceeds 4p_e, and full beats half when 3p_f exceeds 4p_h. With p_e near zero, full wins only if **p_f / (p_f + p_h) > 4/7 = 0.571**, and under complete uncertainty the rule falls down the ladder toward empty. A model that says "full" at 0.6 is therefore only barely justified, and with 81/15/4 class priors (full/half/empty) a model whose p_f is inflated will over-predict full at the -3 price. Boosted trees are known to produce sigmoid-distorted probabilities that Platt scaling or isotonic fixes ([Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana, ICML 2005](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2005/niculescumizil2005icml-predicting)), and Elkan's result is that one should train on the data as given and apply the cost-based decision to the probabilities ([Elkan 2001](https://mlanthology.org/ijcai/2001/elkan2001ijcai-foundations)). The plain reading: the current pipeline already applies the EV rule, so the lever is probability quality at the half/full boundary, particularly p_f.

For that calibration, scikit-learn guidance is that sigmoid is the safer choice on small samples and isotonic overfits below roughly 1,000 points ([scikit-learn calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)). Because the optimum depends only on p_h/p_e and p_f/p_h, the most decision-aligned calibration is two multiplicative odds shifts, one per boundary, tuned on OOF predictions by grid search. Dirichlet calibration (12 parameters for three classes) is the next rung only if reliability diagrams show class-specific bias ([Kull et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656)). Tuning must be nested: scikit-learn's own cost-sensitive example shows threshold tuning on training predictions produces an overfit plateau ([sklearn example](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/model_selection/plot_cost_sensitive_learning.html)), and Cawley and Talbot show selection overfitting can rival differences between algorithms ([JMLR 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html)). `CalibratedClassifierCV` with `ensemble=True` fails when a fold lacks an empty example, which at 4% empty is a live risk, so use custom grouped OOF calibration. Whether ordinal methods beat multiclass plus EV is unproven: the notes found no head-to-head, and OGBoost and gbnet report no gains over standard boosting ([OGBoost](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13456)). A cheap sklearn-only comparison is justified, covering a multiclass baseline, two Frank-Hall binary models for P(y>=half) and P(y>=full) ([Frank and Hall](https://mlanthology.org/ecmlpkdd/2001/frank2001ecml-simple)), and a regression score with two cutpoints tuned for the cost matrix. The plausible mechanism for gain is sharing strength to stabilise the noisy 4% empty class, not accuracy.

Label noise is concentrated exactly where the cost bites. Confident learning ranks suspected label issues from OOF probabilities under a class-conditional noise assumption ([Northcutt et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00068)), and ordinal soft-label work spreads each hard label over neighbouring classes ([Diaz and Marathe, CVPR 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Diaz_Soft_Labels_for_Ordinal_Regression_CVPR_2019_paper.html)). Both are deep-learning-era results with no small-tabular effect sizes. The practical reading, which is my inference, is that one labeller can cheaply re-inspect a few dozen frame-grouped-OOF-flagged borderline areas, and borderline rows can enter a GBM as duplicated rows with fractional weights. Hard-label training on boundary noise should push p_f upward, which is the direction of the observed errors. The same labeller relabelling a random subset blind after a delay gives an intra-annotator ceiling; a model can legitimately exceed measured agreement ([Richie et al., BioNLP 2022](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/2022.bionlp-1.26.pdf)), so near-ceiling scores are not purely model error. Sensei's boundary may also differ from this labeller's, so a boundary-shift sensitivity check on borderline areas is cheap insurance.

## Flash-Lite works best as a reference-anchored, sampled voter

No study evaluates a VLM on shelf or crate fill level with in-context reference crops, so every VLM claim here is adjacent and must be settled on leave-one-frame-out (LOFO) data. The closest evidence is many-shot multimodal in-context learning, where Gemini 1.5 Pro gained about **+17 points on average over zero-shot** across 14 classification datasets, improving roughly log-linearly with examples, with open-weight models showing no such benefit ([Jiang et al., arXiv 2405.09798](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09798v2)). That was Pro, not Flash-Lite, and not a quantity task, so the gain may shrink. The matching bet is a labelled empty/half/full triplet from the same area placed before the query, tested at 0, 1 and 3 exemplars per class, excluding the query frame and its temporal neighbours to respect the leakage rule. A pairwise framing (A = empty reference, B = full reference, C = query) avoids the quadratic cost of pairwise comparison, and swapping A and B gives a free disagreement signal. Evidence for pairwise over absolute is mixed, since only GPT-4V beat single-image evaluation in Q-Bench+ ([arXiv 2402.07116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07116v2)), so it is an A/B arm rather than a default.

Confidence handling is where the evidence is clearest. Verbalised confidence from VLMs, including Gemini Pro Vision, is overconfident ([Groot and Valdenegro-Toro](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.02917)), and probabilistic and consistency signals are more reliable ([arXiv 2508.19111](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19111v1)). In small open VLMs stated confidence sat near 0.87-0.90 with error-detection AUROC near 0.50, while accuracy collapsed from 0.99 to 0.22 under severe underexposure without either signal flagging it ([arXiv 2607.22034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22034), single preprint on non-Gemini models). Logprob availability for the target model is unverified, so test one request before designing around it. Without logprobs, use K=3-5 samples or prompt/order variants, treat vote fractions as p_e, p_h, p_f, shrink them toward the class prior, and feed them to a one-parameter temperature or small multinomial logistic fit under frame-grouped cross-validation. Chain-of-thought is not reliably helpful on perception-bound tasks ([arXiv 2601.04442](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04442v1)), so the schema should put a short observation field (visible crate-floor fraction in 25-point steps, product present, blind down, dark, occluded) before the verdict, and route flagged visibility cases to the temporal and kNN prior rather than trusting the VLM. A zero-shot VLM confusing empty space with low stock is reported by a vendor blog only ([Infosys](https://blogs.infosys.com/topaz/nvidia/enhancing-retail-store-analytics-with-video-ai-agent-real-time-video-analytics-part-3-3.html)), which is weak evidence but matches our crate-floor errors.

The rate limit is the binding constraint, not tokens. Our 256-pixel crops cost 258 tokens each, so a pass over 289 areas is about 150k image tokens, while single-area requests at 15 requests per minute take **19.3 minutes with no retries** ([Gemini image docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-understanding)). Public free-tier figures conflict and the official page defers to the AI Studio account view ([rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)), so the 15 figure comes from the brief and needs a one-request check. Packing 4-6 areas per request gives about 50-72 requests, 3.5-5 minutes per pass, leaving room for three or four samples; batching up to 50 queries per call showed minimal degradation on Pro ([arXiv 2405.09798](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09798v2)), but that is unconfirmed for Flash-Lite and risks cross-area contamination, so compare batched against single on LOFO and keep per-area fallbacks. Rather than calling the VLM everywhere, gate calls by expected regret (max EV minus EV of the current prediction) from the calibrated probabilities and pick the knee of a loss-versus-fraction-deferred curve on grouped OOF data; this is my recommendation, not a published cascade. Upscaling a native 256 crop adds no information and triggers tiling, so prefer sending the crop plus a wider context crop with the target marked.

## Evening drift and sparse frames cap temporal and transductive gains

Every test frame is later than all training frames, so only causal filtering is possible, and the temporal prior is effectively one prediction step from the last labelled frame with uncertainty growing in elapsed time. Kalman and HMM machinery handle irregular spacing by skipping updates, and with no observation the posterior equals the prior ([arXiv 1903.11309](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.11309)). No paper applies HMMs to shelf stock, so the design is inference: a three-state discrete HMM per area with a time-gap-dependent transition (matrix exponential of a generator that mostly depletes, with a small restock rate), pseudo-count priors from the 12-14 labelled frames, per-frame classifier probabilities divided by class priors as emissions, and the **per-area stationary distribution as the fallback** when the gap is hours long. That is consistent with the project's visual-first temporal gating already in place. Change magnitude should gate the prior, but only after photometric normalisation, since sun-angle shadows can overwhelm real changes ([ISPRS 2018](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-3/1673/2018/)) and illumination-invariant change detection is a long-standing topic ([Mester et al.](https://research.uni-luebeck.de/en/publications/illumination-invariant-change-detection-using-a-statistical-colin/)).

For late-evening lighting, the best-supported cheap technique is re-estimating normalisation statistics on target data: swapping in target batch-norm statistics cut ImageNet-C error from 76.7% to 62.2% and helped with as little as 32 samples ([Schneider et al., arXiv 2006.16971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16971)). Entropy-minimising test-time adaptation (Tent) is fragile with small batches, imbalanced labels and one-class collapse ([arXiv 2302.12400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.12400)), so skip it at 26 frames. The feature-space analogue, and the OpenCV one, is per-camera gain or grey-world matching estimated from static regions (shelf frame, floor), never from the stocked area, which would normalise away real stock change. Validate on the last 2-3 frames per camera as an evening proxy. Semi-supervised options are the weakest lever: FixMatch-style gains come from CIFAR-scale data ([arXiv 2001.07685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07685)), confirmation bias is the documented failure ([Arazo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02983v5)), and unlabelled-set shift can degrade SSL ([Oliver et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.09170)). The viable forms are kNN-kernel `LabelSpreading` ([scikit-learn guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/semi_supervised.html)) and one round of self-training that adds a test crop only when the VLM and the pixel classifier agree, with per-class top-K selection to avoid pseudo-labelling only the majority class ([UPL](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2204.03649v1)). Within-area k=2-3 clustering over train plus test crops, anchored to labelled frames rather than cluster size, is plausible but unvalidated by any source, and clusters can split on product swaps such as bananas in an oranges area rather than on stock.

## A 289-area test set only resolves large gains

Evaluation discipline decides whether any of this can be believed. Random cross-validation across frames of the same area leaks and underestimates error ([Roberts et al., Ecography 2017](https://pred.uni-regensburg.de/id/eprint/1455/)), and leakage routes here include kNN and temporal features, calibration scored on its own tuning predictions, VLM prompts or fusion weights tuned on the labelled frames, and label cleaning using models that saw the target frame. The existing forward-in-time score is the right outer estimate; keep tuning nested inside it. For a 90% binary rate on 289 areas the standard error is 1.76 points, so a 95% interval is about **plus or minus 3.5 points**, wider once clustering over 26 images and 14 cameras is counted (my arithmetic; standard statistics, uncited). With roughly 12 empty and 43 half areas expected, zero errors on 12 empties still leaves a rule-of-three upper bound near 25%, so per-class claims from the hidden set are unsupported. Compare models with paired cluster bootstrap on per-area score differences, and treat gains below about one standard error of the LOFO score as noise, as Oliver et al. warn for small validation sets. Cluster bootstrap is needed because ordinary resampling under-covers when within-cluster correlation exists ([Iowa State repository](https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/entities/publication/715dec91-9bb1-4f5c-9b3a-ef24bfd2e1fc)).

## Conclusion

The central finding is that the stock deficit is a calibration-and-reference problem rather than a model-capacity problem. The cost matrix makes full the riskiest answer, the observed errors are all in that direction, and the literature's one portable signal (occupied fraction versus a per-area reference) targets precisely the visible crate floor that fools the current system. Where the research is silent is as informative as where it speaks: no source validates ordinal methods over multiclass plus EV, VLM reference crops for fill, per-area clustering, HMMs for stock or lighting adaptation on retail cameras, so each is an experiment gated on LOFO, not a known win. The unresearched foundation-embedding topic remains the largest open question and could change the ranking below, because a good frozen backbone would strengthen the reference-bank, kNN and label-spreading items simultaneously.
